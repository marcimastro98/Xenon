Fan control without fan curves

Airflow is the part of Xenon that drives your PC's fans. There are no curves to draw: pick a mode and a step between Quietest and Coolest, and Xenon learns your PC and keeps the fans as quiet as the temperatures allow.

Three steps, a few minutes

Open Xenon → Fans. Airflow stays off until you turn it on. First a fan check of a few minutes finds which control moves which fan and how slow each one can go. The fans are loud while it runs, and you can stop it.

Then Xenon watches for as long as you like: it shows what it would do and about how much quieter the PC would be, without touching the fans. When you are happy, let it drive.

Fans first says what it found, place by place, so you can check it against your case: how many fan headers the motherboard has and how many have something turning, how many fan controls the graphics card has, and whether there is an iCUE LINK hub.

A mode, then quiet or cool

Pick Automatic, Quiet, Balanced, Cool or Back to the PC, then under Quiet or cool pick one of five steps from Quietest to Coolest. Automatic chooses by what the PC is doing: quiet at rest, ready when a game starts.

Every change says why, in the Xenon menu and on the Fans widget: the processor at work, a game starting, something too hot. When the fans speed up enough to be heard, a pop-up says why once, and names the program behind it when it can.

Bit, Deck keys and a paired phone can change the mode too: ask Bit to make the fans quieter, or why they sped up.

Why not a fan curve

A fan curve ties one temperature to one speed, so the fans react after the heat has arrived and chase every short spike. Xenon reads the load, which rises before the temperature, so it moves the fans earlier and more gently.

It lets short peaks pass, keeps matching fans at the same speed so they do not hum against each other, gets ready when a game it has seen starts, and can stay quiet during a call or at night. With a liquid cooler it follows the liquid, which warms slowly, against the air the radiator breathes.

What it drives on each system

SystemFans Xenon can drive
WindowsThe motherboard's fan headers, through the Xenon Sensors service; NVIDIA graphics cards, whose fans still stop on their own when the card is cool; a CORSAIR iCUE LINK hub with its fans and pump, while iCUE is closed
Windows (Beta)AMD graphics cards, and USB coolers the sensor library knows: NZXT Kraken and its fan hub, MSI CoreLiquid, ARCTIC, Razer
Linux (Beta)Any fan chip the kernel can drive, AMD graphics cards and coolers with a kernel driver, after one command Xenon shows you; the iCUE LINK hub too. NVIDIA cards are read only
Mac and laptopsNot available: they drive their own fans

A model Xenon has never tried on real hardware is marked Beta and is driven only after the fan check has seen its fan go back to the PC when Xenon lets go. Any fan can be left to the PC.

Safety comes first

If something runs too hot, every fan goes to full speed until it cools. A temperature that stops arriving, a fan that stops when it should spin, or Xenon going silent for 5 seconds hands those fans back to the PC's own settings, and so do quitting Xenon, updating, sleep and shutdown.

A pump never stops and never runs under half speed. The Health tab follows worn fans and dust, and a new processor or graphics card starts that part's learning again by itself.

With iCUE, NZXT CAM or Fan Control

Only one program should drive each fan. If iCUE, NZXT CAM, Fan Control or MSI Afterburner drives the same fans, leave those fans to the PC in Xenon or close that app. Xenon drives an iCUE LINK hub only while iCUE is closed, and lets go if iCUE takes it.

On Windows the motherboard's fans are reached through LibreHardwareMonitor and PawnIO, the signed driver Fan Control uses too: what PawnIO is and why Xenon installs it.

Common questions

Is Airflow free?

Yes. Fan control is part of Xenon, which is free and needs no account.

Does it work on a laptop or a Mac?

No. Laptops and Macs drive their own fans, so Xenon leaves them alone.

Can Xenon drive iCUE LINK fans without iCUE?

Yes, on Windows and as Beta on Linux, with iCUE closed: tap Use the LINK hub in Fans, or turn on Drive the iCUE LINK hub in Fans, Options. Its fans and pump join in, and Xenon lights them itself. Turn it off and the hub goes straight back to its own saved settings.

What if I don't like what Xenon does?

Pick Back to the PC, or turn off Let Xenon drive the fans, and the fans go back to the PC's own settings. Any single fan can be left to the PC too.
