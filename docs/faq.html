What Xenon is

What is Xenon?

A free app that puts widgets for your PC on any screen: temperatures and load, FPS, music, calendar, weather and notes, on your desktop, over your games, on a spare monitor, a tablet or a phone. It also has the Deck, touch keys that run things on the PC, a screen recorder with Instant Replay, Bit, an assistant you can talk to, and a Store of themes and widgets. It runs on Windows, with macOS and Linux in beta.

Do I need a Xeneon Edge or a CORSAIR PC?

No. Xenon started on the CORSAIR Xeneon Edge, but it runs on any PC and needs no CORSAIR hardware or software. On an Edge it works with or without iCUE: you only need iCUE to show Xenon inside an iCUE widget or to drive CORSAIR RGB devices. The Xeneon Edge guide has the details.

Which screen can I use?

The one you have. On a single monitor the widgets sit on your desktop or over a game, and you choose where each one goes. A spare monitor or a touchscreen can hold full pages of widgets: the app asks which screen to use the first time it runs. A tablet or a phone works too, in beta, over your own Wi-Fi: it shows the same layout, widgets and theme, and its keys really run on the PC. The one-screen guide, the phone guide and the tablet guide show how.

Does it work on Mac and Linux?

Yes, in beta: macOS 11 or later (one download for Apple Silicon and Intel) and 64-bit Linux, next to Windows 10 and 11. The widgets and the Store are the same on all three. Some sensors and system actions are still missing on macOS and Linux, and a widget with no data on your system says so instead of loading forever. The Mac and Linux pages list what works.

First launch on a Mac: from 5.0 the app is notarized by Apple, so macOS only asks once whether to open an app downloaded from the internet, and you click Open. If macOS says it cannot check Xenon, that copy is older than 5.0, so download the latest .dmg.

Price and supporters

Is it free? What do supporters get?

The app is free, with no ads and no account. Supporters on Buy Me a Coffee get the monthly drops in the catalog, the Lyrics and Developer cleanup widgets, Maker export, a role on the Discord and a place in the Hall of Supporters. It is 5 USD a month or 50 USD a year, or once: 5 USD for one month, 25 USD for five months, 50 USD for twelve months. A payment under 5 USD is a plain tip: it opens nothing and brings no Sparks. The code arrives by email and goes in Settings → Support Xenon. Every plan also brings 300 Sparks a month, Xenon's credits. Unused ones do not carry over.

When your support ends you keep what you unlocked, its updates and the Sparks you bought, and Lyrics and Developer cleanup stay unlocked. You lose Maker export and the monthly Sparks. A year of support also gets you a theme made for you from your palette or a photo, published with your name on it.

Can I try the supporter pass first?

Yes: ask for it with your email address and the code arrives by email. It is free for 7 days, once per PC, with no card, and it opens the supporter drops and Lyrics. Sparks and Developer cleanup are not part of the trial, new drops usually join after their first 30 days, and limited drops need their own code. When the trial ends, what you tried comes off the PC, your earlier theme returns and your widgets keep their place.

What are Sparks?

Sparks are Xenon's credits: 1 Spark is 1 US cent. You spend them on Bit when you use it without an API key of your own, and on the Store items sold for Sparks. Every month of support includes 300, spent first, which reset at the start of each month and do not carry over. You do not need to be a supporter to buy more: from Xenon 5.0.0 the packs are in the Store, under Sparks, 500 Sparks for 5 USD, 1050 for 10 USD or 2200 for 20 USD, and bought Sparks never expire. If you are not a supporter, a Sparks code (XC-…) arrives by email with your first pack: it goes in the same box as the supporter code.

An item bought with Sparks is yours on up to 3 PCs, with every update published for it, and installing it again costs nothing. Limited editions are never sold for Sparks.

Can I get a refund?

Xenon is the seller; Buy Me a Coffee takes the payment. Within 14 days of paying you can get a membership payment or a Sparks pack refunded: write to support@xenon-app.com and the refund goes through Buy Me a Coffee. A refund removes the pack and what you bought with those Sparks. Sparks already spent on Bit count against your balance, which can go below zero until your next pack.

A Store item bought with Sparks is delivered at once. Before buying you tick a box asking for that, and the right to withdraw ends when delivery starts.

Using it

Does it slow down my games?

It is built not to. While a game is running, Game mode fades out the animated background and pauses animated effects, and the heaviest widgets, the Browser and Second screen streams, stop until you finish. Xenon's own background work, such as disk scans, waits too. Live widgets like those and Home Assistant only stay connected while they are on screen.

On Windows, touching Xenon on another screen mid-game is safe: the cursor goes straight back to the game's monitor and the tap does not take the game's focus.

Can anti-cheat flag it or get me banned?

Xenon never injects code into a game and never hooks its process. It draws its own windows on top and reads frame times from the system with PresentMon, so there is nothing inside the game for anti-cheat to flag.

The only part that works at driver level is PawnIO, which reads temperatures and stays outside the game. FACEIT's anti-cheat blocked PawnIO versions before 2.1.0: if it names PawnIO.sys, run winget upgrade namazso.PawnIO. Not every anti-cheat has been tested: if one complains, say so on the Discord.

Can it show temperatures and in-game FPS?

Yes. On Windows, Install sensors in the Fans or Energy widget sets up temperature reading (it needs the administrator prompt once), and PresentMon gives the real frame rate of the running game, fullscreen titles included; Settings → Performance → Game mode installs it if it is missing. The FPS overlay shows it over the game, in borderless or windowed full screen. On macOS temperatures and GPU load come from macmon (brew install vladkens/tap/macmon). On Linux, in-game FPS needs MangoHud, with the game launched through it.

Can it record my games?

Yes. Instant Replay keeps the last minutes, so you can save a moment after it happened, and the recorder takes the screen or one window. It also takes screenshots and reads text off the screen. Everything is saved on your PC, nothing is uploaded, and there is no account. It is free on Windows, and in beta on macOS and Linux. The recorder guide shows how.

What is Bit? Do I need an API key?

Bit is the assistant inside Xenon. You type to it or say “Hey Bit”, and it answers, acts on the PC and makes widgets with you. You need no key to start: a new install gets 30 trial Sparks at the first message, for Bit only, good for 7 days, with no card and no email. After that you can buy Sparks, or use your own key for Gemini, OpenAI or Anthropic, a local model through Ollama or an OpenAI-compatible server, or Claude Code and Codex on your own subscription.

Can it replace a Stream Deck?

It does a similar job on a screen you already own: a Xeneon Edge, a spare monitor, a tablet or a phone. A key can open an app, send a shortcut, switch an OBS scene, mute Discord or run a Streamer.bot action, and it can light up to show the state of what it controls. It does not use Stream Deck hardware or plugins. The Deck guide lists every action.

How do I add widgets and themes?

Press + on a page to add a built-in widget. To put a widget on your desktop or over a game, press Alt+Shift+X for the Xenon menu, or ask Bit. For more, open Settings → Store: themes, backgrounds, widgets, ambient scenes and packs, each with a preview before anything is applied, installed with one tap. You can browse the same things in the catalog on this site.

Can I make my own widget or theme?

Yes, without code. Maker, in beta, builds widgets and themes in the browser, and Bit makes them with you there or in the app. Trying everything in Maker is free; keeping projects and installing what you make are for supporters. If you write code, the creator guide covers the Widget SDK.

Does it work with Spotify, Home Assistant and RGB lights?

Yes, each one optional and off until you connect it.

  • Spotify: whatever you play shows in the Playback widget. Link your account in Settings to add seek, like, shuffle and playlists, also from the Deck (playback control needs Spotify Premium).
  • Home Assistant: connect it in Settings → Smart Home with your address and a long-lived token, then control lights, sensors and scenes from a widget or a Deck key.
  • RGB: CORSAIR devices through iCUE, plus WLED, OpenRGB, Philips Hue, Nanoleaf, Govee, LIFX and Yeelight over your network, and GIGABYTE and AORUS motherboards over USB on Windows.
Can I save my layout or move it to another PC?

Yes. Settings → General → Backup → Export backup saves one file with your layout, Deck, calendar, tasks, timers, notes, settings, Bit's memory and custom background. On the other PC, Import backup puts it all back. Passwords, tokens and API keys are never in the file, so integrations ask you to connect again, and community widgets are listed so you can reinstall them from their codes. Private calendar links and Deck webhooks are in it, so keep the file private.

When something goes wrong

Windows says Xenon is a virus. Is it safe?

It is a false positive. The Windows installer has been signed since version 4.11.9, but Microsoft builds trust in a new file over time, so Defender, SmartScreen or your browser can still warn about a fresh release. You can check the file yourself: every release ships a signed SHA256SUMS list, and the download page shows the one command to compare it. You can also install Xenon from the Microsoft Store.

  • Defender removed it: open Windows Security → Virus & threat protection → Protection history, pick the Xenon entry and choose Restore. Then add %LOCALAPPDATA%\Xenon and %LOCALAPPDATA%\Programs\Xenon under Exclusions.
  • The browser blocks the download: open the downloads list with Ctrl+J and choose Keep.
  • It does not start and nothing was reported: on Windows 11 that is usually Smart App Control, under Windows Security → App & browser control. Try the latest release with it still on first: an up-to-date Windows lets you turn it back on later, but on an older one it stays off until you reset or reinstall the PC.

Only bypass a warning for a file from the official Releases page whose hash you checked.

Xenon closes on its own

Open the tray menu and choose Open crash log. If the last line is a PANIC, the app failed by itself: paste that line on the Discord or in a bug report, it names the exact spot. If the last line is launched with nothing after it, something outside the app closed it, almost always the antivirus: look in Protection history for an entry at that moment, restore it and add the two exclusions from the question above.

I can’t see the CPU temperature

On Windows, press Install sensors in the Fans or Energy widget, or in Settings → Performance, and accept the administrator prompt: reading temperatures needs LibreHardwareMonitor and PawnIO, which include a driver-level part, and a small service that reads them for Xenon, and that is what the prompt is for. Xenon itself keeps running with normal rights. Decline it and everything else still works, just without temperatures. On macOS, install macmon. Some laptops and boards expose no sensor at all; the widget then says so instead of showing an invented number.

How do I update or uninstall it?

Xenon updates itself: when a release is out it asks, one tap downloads it, checks its signature before installing, and puts the previous version back if anything fails. Your settings, layouts and notes stay.

To uninstall on Windows, open Settings → Apps → Installed apps, find Xenon and press Uninstall. On macOS double-click UNINSTALL.command in ~/Library/Application Support/Xenon, and on Linux run ./UNINSTALL.sh from the Xenon folder. Both remove the app, the startup entry and your data; to keep your settings, layouts and notes, run UNINSTALL.sh --keep-data.

Privacy and help

Does it send my data anywhere? Do I need an account?

Your settings, notes, tasks, events, Deck and file index stay on your PC, and the app keeps no log of what you do. It has three anonymous counters, with no identifier: the version, system and rough age of the install, sent with the update check; 1 added to a creation's install count when you install it from the Store; and once a day how many times each feature ran and failed. None of them says what you search for, type or make. On a new install they start on, and you can turn each one off in Settings → General → Updates.

The app also goes online to check for updates and fetch a short list of announcements, and for the services you turn on yourself, like the weather or Spotify. What you ask Bit goes to the AI service you chose; with a local model it stays on your PC. The privacy page lists every call it can make and what it sends.

There is no Xenon account. You enter a code only if you support Xenon, buy Sparks or ask for the trial, which needs an email address, and integrations use their own logins.

Where do I get help?

Ask on the Discord: it is the fastest, and questions that keep coming back end up on this page. For a bug you can repeat, open a GitHub issue with your system, what you did and what happened.

Personal use is free, including on the PC you work on. Selling Xenon or shipping it inside a commercial product needs written permission first: write to support@xenon-app.com.
